01 — Derivatives Pricing

Options Pricing Engine AI-Assisted

Multi-model derivatives pricing library with five interoperable engines: Black-Scholes closed-form with analytical Greeks, a CRR binomial tree (European and American), Monte Carlo with antithetic and control variates plus pathwise Greeks, a Crank-Nicolson PDE solver verified at second-order spatial convergence, and Heston stochastic volatility via Gil-Pelaez inversion, benchmarked to Albrecher et al. (2007) Table 2 to four significant figures. 53 passing pytest checks cover put-call parity, characteristic-function identities, and Black-Scholes-limit degeneracy.

In other words

Five independent implementations of the same quantity, cross-validated against each other and against published reference values, with automated tests for the identities that must hold.

Stack Python, NumPy, SciPy, stochastic calculus, PDE methods, Monte Carlo, pytest

02 — Forecast Evaluation

Equity Premium Forecasting

Reproduction of Rapach, Strauss & Zhou (2010)

Out-of-sample reproduction of the Rapach-Strauss-Zhou combination-forecast framework: expanding-window OLS across 15 macroeconomic predictors, five combination schemes (mean, median, trimmed mean, and DMSPE at two discount factors), each evaluated against the historical-average benchmark. The reproduction matched the published Table 1 for 14 of 15 individual predictors; combination forecasts delivered an out-of-sample R² of 3.6%, reproducing the paper's central result that predictors which fail individually can succeed in aggregate.

Methodology

The model never sees data from after the point it forecasts, and every result is measured against a simple benchmark rather than reported on its own. Validation is by checking the output against the published table.

Stack R, time series, out-of-sample evaluation, forecast combination

03 — Market Data & Implied Volatility

Volatility Surface Lab AI-Assisted In Progress

Constructing implied volatility surfaces from market option chains. Built so far: a schema-validated data pipeline with composable liquidity filters over committed SPY chain snapshots, and implied-volatility extraction by Brent's method. The project is not finished.

Data handling

Third-party market data is ingested through validation and filters before any modeling happens.

Why this one connects to the thesis

Multifractal volatility modeling is one of the settings where log-correlated fields show up, and the estimation question in my research — how far a scaling exponent measured from a finite record can be trusted — is the same question that decides what a fitted surface can actually say. This project is where that constraint becomes empirical rather than theoretical.

Stack Python, NumPy, SciPy, pandas
